## 3. Article guidance

All six records are citable; none carries a refutation, correction, retraction, or cancellation signal.

- **airbnb-airchat — cite with the strongest caveats of this set.** The 64% PR-share figure is third-party-conference-sourced (October 2025 DPE Summit talk by Airbnb engineers Szczepan Faber and Mike Nakhimovich; recording 2025-10-15), with no first-party airbnb.tech post as of 2026-10-08. Word it as: "about 64% of PRs materialized through agentic coding over a nine-month period, per an October 2025 conference talk by Airbnb engineers." For current state, prefer the first-party "97% of Airbnb's weekly PR authors now use agentic AI, and nobody mandated it" (2026-10-03). Never conflate the bases: 64% of PRs ≠ 97% of weekly PR authors ≠ ~59% of code primarily AI-authored ≠ 60% of new code (earnings-call claim via TechCrunch paraphrase) ≠ +65% PR throughput. Do not cite the LinkedIn corroboration (search snippets only, posts unfetchable).
- **doordash-code-review — safe; first-party engineering blog.** Give both volume figures with units and dates: "more than 10,000 pull requests across 56 onboarded repositories in a typical week (May 2026)" and "more than 25,000 automated code reviews each week (August 2026)" — and state the units differ (review runs vs pull requests). Quote the article's actual sentence; the "4,000 developers / 60% of diffs" tail is not in it. If quoting mechanism, use "soft and hard timeouts".
- **sentry-junior — safe.** Cite David Cramer, "Building an intern" (cra.mr, 2026-07-02) — not Ronacher. Date the size figure: "~100,000 lines of TypeScript as of July 2026" (or "~171,700 by October 2026" if the updated count is cataloged). Internal use is first-party attested; the repo is public, Apache-2.0, and actively released.
- **sierra-pinecone — safe; first-party.** Keep the scope attached: 600 people created more than 75,000 sessions in the month preceding 2026-07-15, Sierra employees; the same period scopes "96% of engineering now uses Pinecone" and "70% of our PRs were opened through Pinecone". Do not use FourWeekMBA's "since March" — a secondary mislabel.
- **dropbox-nova — safe.** Attach "dozens of agents" to what the source says: migration owners "could launch and manage dozens of agents with the same runbook", which Nova productizes as prompt-once parallel execution — not a Nova spec. If using the adoption figure, say "roughly 1 in 12 pull requests at Dropbox as of May 2026".
- **databricks-costar — safe with a scope caveat.** Say coSTAR ships and tests agents "including internal engineering workflows such as on-call support and automated code reviewers" — the methodology also covers customer-facing and OSS agents. Do not juxtapose coSTAR's LLM judges with the benchmarking post's judge skepticism without noting they are different contexts. Keep Omnigent and coSTAR clearly separate offerings.
